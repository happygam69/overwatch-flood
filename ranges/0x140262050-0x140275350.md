## What this range is for (reading conclusion)

Everything in pass 13 is still **static initialization / registration machinery**, but the mix
flipped: passes 8–12 were builder-sea-dominated with occasional registration blocks; pass 13 is a
151-row copy-wrapper sea (each row = one named object whose root is `&DAT_143d013b0` in 112 cases
or one of 13 other class roots) followed by a compact, self-contained registration block that
ships (a) 22 registrations in a sixth output band, (b) five `{id,hash}` lookup-table rows
(including the first two-ids-one-hash case), (c) the two sorted-insert builders and one
two-buffer builder that consume registration outputs directly, and (d) a chain of rootless
builders (365/479/231/377-B) that are rooted on registration outputs — i.e. the "class" objects
of the builder sea are, in this block, the products of the registrations. Evidence for that last
point: every rootless builder's 2-arg `c2d70` source equals a registration arg1 from the same
chunk (`0x143de2e50`, `0x143de30f0`, `0x143de3670`, `0x143de4530/45f0/4830/48f0`,
`&DAT_143dc6920` for the 558-B). [the −0x20 rule is measured; the "products" reading is
inference from the wiring]
